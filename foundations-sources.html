Divine Sources & Study

Every Technique belongs to a supernatural Source. In the recognized traditions, those Sources are gods—but the system is built around learned practice, not inherited elemental gifts.

A god’s Techniques reflect its nature, history, domain, mythology, and traditions. That influence shapes the curriculum; it does not reduce a practitioner to a generic category such as “fire user” or “lightning user.” A fighter chooses which traditions to study, learns specific Techniques, and trains their body and equipment around them.

Study is the real gate

Techniques must be learned and trained. A person who wants a divine ability first studies the god to which that ability belongs, then learns how to use it safely and effectively. Combat ability is always a compound result:

  • the Techniques a practitioner has learned,
  • their physical ability and martial training,
  • their Templates, Symbols, and loadout preparation,
  • their timing, observation, and creativity, and
  • their accumulated experience under pressure.

Foundational distinction

Gods provide the available Techniques. They do not automatically provide the judgement to deploy them well. Skill remains personal.

One tradition or many

A practitioner may study multiple gods. No rule prohibits it. The cost is intellectual and practical complexity: each Source brings an enormous body of knowledge, methods, risks, symbols, and combat habits. Trying to learn every Technique of every god is like attempting to master every university subject that has ever existed.

ApproachStrengthPressure
Deep specialistExceptional command of one tradition; refined personal applications.Fewer divine toolsets immediately available.
Broad practitionerCan carry distinct Templates from distinct Sources and solve problems from several directions.Must master and integrate several difficult traditions.

Neither path automatically makes someone stronger. A lifelong specialist may understand one divine tradition with astonishing depth. A broader student may create a flexible style through combinations across separate Templates. The decisive factor is not the number of gods studied, but the quality of the practitioner’s integration.

A useful correction

People do not have a god’s power. They learn a god’s Techniques—and then decide what to do with them.
