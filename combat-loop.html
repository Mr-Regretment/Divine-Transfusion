Combat Practice

The Combat Loop

Infusion fights are iterative. A prepared Template is deployed, observed, activated, emptied, reloaded, abandoned, recovered, or replaced—often in seconds.

The standard loop

  1. Choose — select a Template and single-Source loadout.
  2. Position — arrange body, carrier, angle, and anticipated origin points.
  3. Deploy — throw, hold, place, wear, or otherwise move the Template into the field.
  4. Wait — let the opponent react, expose information, or enter the desired geometry.
  5. Activate — trigger one or more stored Techniques.
  6. Sequence — use the remaining effects with timing, order, and physical events in mind.
  7. Reload — refill empty Slots from prepared Symbols where possible.
  8. Adapt — change carriers, exploit new placement, or abandon the original plan.

Why preparation matters

A practitioner expecting conflict can prepare Templates, spare Symbols, Symbol films, blood-bound personal equipment, multiple divine loadouts, backup carriers, and deliberately placed Slots. Preparation creates options but does not turn them into decisions. Every prepared advantage still needs timing and execution.

What an opponent tries to force

  • Disarm the practitioner or capture a carrier.
  • Survive long enough for an authority transition.
  • Destroy the ordinary carrier, scatter the battlefield, or steal the durable Slots.
  • Read the geometry, predict the activation, or force a Symbol to be wasted.
  • Make the user reload in an exposed or inconvenient moment.

The fight changes after every activation

Each consumed Symbol changes the options on the field. An exhausted Slot, a moved carrier, a newly visible hidden plate, or a changing authority timer may matter more than raw destructive power.

The high-speed version

Masters do not necessarily gain extra rules. They become able to run more of the existing loop simultaneously: movement, trajectory, activation, reloading, orientation, authority, timing, and opponent position all at once.
