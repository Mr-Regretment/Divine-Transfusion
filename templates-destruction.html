Destruction & Recovery

The carrier can fail. The occupied Spell Slot is the part designed to endure.

Occupied Slots are extraordinarily durable. Within the limits of modern technology, destroying one is effectively impossible. The surrounding Template may burn, shatter, bend, or be smashed apart while the loaded Slot survives. This is a defining structural asymmetry: the ordinary object is expendable; the magical connection is not.

After a carrier breaks

If a Template is destroyed but its Slots remain intact, the Slots retain their stored Techniques. They can be carried as independent objects, transported, recovered by another party, or installed into a new Template. The surviving Slot is not merely salvage—it is still a loaded magical component.

Recover / Reinstall

The durable Slot retains its stored Symbol until activation. It may outlive the carrier many times over.

Stealing Slots

A Symbol cannot be removed by itself; trying to separate it damages or removes the entire Slot. The entire Slot, however, can be taken out of its carrier. This makes loaded Slots valuable targets for theft, recovery, ransom, and battlefield scavenging. Destroying a weapon does not necessarily deny its stored Technique to an enemy.

Combat consequence

Breaking an opponent’s carrier may create a worse problem: a small, nearly indestructible, loaded object on the battlefield. It can be retrieved, carried independently, or mounted into a new Template.

Self-destructive designs

A Technique can affect its own Template. There is no universal protection against a spell moving, damaging, exploding, or otherwise transforming the object that bears it. Practitioners can deliberately design disposable or self-modifying carriers around that fact. The Slot may remain after the rest is gone, ready to be recovered and used again.
