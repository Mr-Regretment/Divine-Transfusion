Forbidden Sources & Modern Infusion

Infusion belongs to a living world of temples, laboratories, inherited traditions, controversial Sources, and modern manufacturing. Magic does not replace technology.

Forbidden Sources

Not every supernatural being is recognised as a legitimate god. Some are classified as Forbidden Sources: demons, monsters, dangerous entities, or beings associated with South American folklore and other traditions. A Forbidden Source may still possess Techniques that can be learned and stored, creating dangerous and politically charged schools of practice.

Societies may disagree about whether an entity is a god, demon, monster, ancestor, spirit, or something else. The system’s mechanical restriction remains steady: one Template can only contain Techniques from one Source. The label changes the institution, taboo, and danger—not the underlying compatibility rule.

Forbidden does not mean imaginary

It means access is contested, hazardous, prohibited, or morally charged. The Techniques may still be real, learnable, and fieldable by someone willing to accept the consequences.

Cultural foundations

Infusion suits a setting with strong South American and Colombian cultural influences. Regions can develop distinct gods, temple networks, symbols, schools, techniques, objects, and martial traditions. Traditional objects can become contemporary Templates; ancient divine techniques can inhabit modern fighting practice without becoming museum pieces.

The two modes do not erase each other; they create a world in which preservation, industrial access, regulation, and cultural authority all matter.
