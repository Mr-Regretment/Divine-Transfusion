Loadouts, Geometry & Deception

A Template is a limited combat loadout. Its object, Source, three Techniques, placement, order, and activation plan all communicate—or conceal—a strategy.

The loadout questions

What object?
Choose a carrier whose normal use, movement, material, and vulnerability suit the plan.
What Source?
The first Symbol commits the entire Template to one god or Source.
What three Techniques?
Decide which three single-use tools justify the available slots.
What order?
Build a retained installation sequence that supports an intended chain.
Where are the Slots?
Define each effect’s origin point and its interaction with orientation and movement.
When do they act?
Plan for release, flight, impact, landing, catch, or a delayed opening.
Who has authority?
Account for the active user, the transition timer, and any blood-bound exception.
How will it be misread?
Use hidden Symbols, false assumptions, and ordinary appearance to control information.

Designing interaction without inventing a new spell

The most advanced combinations do not need to fuse Techniques. One effect can physically launch or expose another carrier; a throw can alter the origin point; an explosion can reposition a knife before its next Symbol is triggered; a concealed Slot can turn an apparently spent object into a new threat. The combination lives in the relationship between independent effects and real motion.

Read objects as plans

A balanced fighter studies the carrier as carefully as the visible Symbol. A Slot at the rear of a knife, a plate hidden under a glove cuff, or a suspiciously replaceable piece of armor can reveal more than a straightforward attack.

Information advantage

The opponent does not always know whether a Template is loaded, how many Slots it has, whether a visible Symbol is genuine bait, whether a concealed Symbol exists, where an effect will originate, or whether the user retains authority. This uncertainty is a core part of the system, not an incidental trick.
