Activation, Range & Control

A practitioner who has authority over a loaded Symbol chooses when to activate its stored Technique. Touching the Template is not required.

Timing is a skill

Activation is not tethered to the moment a Template leaves the hand. A practitioner can trigger a stored Technique as the object is released, while it is moving, when it reaches a target, after it lands, after another person catches it, or at another tactically appropriate moment.

ReleaseOn departureUse the first instant of a throw, strike, or placement.
TransitIn motionExploit orientation, velocity, or a target’s reaction.
AftermathOn landing or captureTurn a missed or surrendered object into a delayed threat.

Activation range

The practitioner must be able to see the Template they intend to activate and remain within the system’s effective range: approximately half a kilometre, subject to that visual requirement. In practice, the user needs an unbroken enough visual relationship to the carrier and must not be too far from it.

Range in one sentence

No line of sight, no activation. Clear sight but beyond roughly half a kilometre, no activation. The user does not need to be touching the Template.

Control follows authority

Possession alone is not activation control. Ordinary loaded Symbols follow the active user after the transition period; blood-bound Symbols stay with their creator. The practitioner must therefore consider line of sight, distance, and authority all at once. A hidden dagger across the room can be a live piece of a fight—if its rightful controller can still see it.

Movement creates opportunities

Because activation can occur after deployment, a Template’s changing position becomes part of the intended effect. Throws, rebounds, falls, catches, and deliberate placements can all establish a more advantageous origin point before a Symbol is spent.
