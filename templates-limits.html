Templates & the Three-Slot Limit

A Template is an ordinary object prepared with Spell Slots. It is a platform for a loadout, not an inherently magical artifact.

Templates can be made from steel, porcelain, wood, plastic, fabric, and other suitable materials. They may be weapons, tools, jewelry, clothes, armor, small objects, projectiles, specialist equipment, or even parts of a living body. A blank Template is compatible with Techniques from any Source.

The absolute limit

Non-negotiable rule

Every Template can contain a maximum of three Spell Slots.

The limit is absolute. A large object does not receive more Slots. A more accomplished practitioner does not receive more Slots. A legendary object does not receive more Slots. A Template may have one, two, or three Slots—and any emptied Slot remains a Slot, available to be refilled.

A Template can contain fewer than three Slots. It can never contain a fourth.

Why scarcity is useful

Three spaces turn every Template into a tactical statement. The wielder must decide what object to use, which Source claims it, which three Techniques deserve the spaces, where each Slot sits, what order the Symbols entered, and when each should activate. A theoretically powerful Technique may be less valuable than one that fits the carrier and fighting style precisely.

A loadout, not a spell list

Infusion does not ask “what can this person cast?” It asks “what did they prepare in these three physical locations, and why?”

Empty is not absent

Once a Symbol is consumed, the Template loses the stored use but not the structural potential. Its empty Slot can receive a new Symbol later. This is why a small set of well-designed Templates can support many changing configurations through a long engagement.
